# B43 - Follow a click into the tab it opens

## What was built

When a click opens a new tab, the agent now moves into it.

## Key decisions

The failed step was a successful click. Internshala opens a listing in a new tab, the driver tracked the tab but stayed on the results page, and the verifier compared the results page's URL before and after, saw no change, and failed the step. Two earlier choices combined badly: tabs open without moving the agent, and verification reads the active tab. Rather than teach the verifier about other tabs, the driver now behaves as a person does and follows the click, which makes the page the agent sees match the page the click produced. The earlier rule was a deliberate decision (switching is the planner's job), so the reversal is recorded here and in the changelog, and the planner's prompt tells it the page it came from is still open.

The new tab is checked against the allowed domains before the agent works in it. A tab that fails the check is closed and the agent goes back, because a page outside the sandbox must not become the page the agent acts on.

## Viva questions

**1. Why change the driver instead of the verifier?**
The planner chooses its next action from the page it is shown. If the agent stays on the old tab it plans against the wrong page even when verification is made smarter. Following the click fixes what is verified and what is planned from.

**2. Why wait up to three seconds for some clicks and 0.4 for others?**
A link that declares `target=_blank` will open a tab, so waiting is certain to pay off. A plain click almost never does, so it only gets a quick look, for a script that opens a window, and costs a fraction of a second.

**3. Why close a disallowed tab instead of just not switching to it?**
Leaving it open would keep a page outside the sandbox alive in the task's browser. Closing it and reporting the breach keeps the allow-list a rule rather than a suggestion.
